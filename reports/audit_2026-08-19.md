## 2. Bugs found

### 2.1 Truth substitution in `cf_sample_complexity.py` — FIXED

`experiments/cf_sample_complexity.py:135`, inside `predict()`:

```python
# observed cells are known exactly, for every estimator alike
P[M] = Yobs[M]
```

**Root cause.** Pasting ground truth into the observed cells before the `argmin` makes the
"fixed ranking" arm no longer a fixed ranking: its within-row ordering changes wherever truth was
substituted. It therefore stops instantiating the additive ceiling, and H2 — the script's restatement
of Proposition 1 as a sample-complexity claim — is no longer being tested. The tell is that the
arm's regret *varied with density*, which a genuine fixed ranking's cannot except through its fitted
column effects.

**Fix.** Removed the line; every estimator is scored on its own fitted surface.

| density | CF before | CF after | fixed before | fixed after | row-mean before | row-mean after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 % | 1.0813 | 1.0830 | 1.0764 | 1.0792 | 1.5031 | 1.8185 |
| 30 % | 1.0257 | 1.0322 | 1.0235 | 1.0258 | 1.3711 | 1.8175 |
| 70 % | 1.0099 | **1.0206** | 1.0112 | 1.0233 | 1.1457 | 1.8159 |
| 90 % | 1.0088 | **1.0203** | 1.0107 | 1.0228 | 1.0668 | 1.7603 |

The script's headline changes: "density needed to reach 101 % of oracle energy — bilinear CF: 70 %"
becomes **"not reached in sweep"**. All six checks still pass, now for the right reason.

**Paper impact: none.** No paper sentence traces to this file; `sample-complexity` appears in the
paper only in Related Work, citing reference [25].

### 2.3 The additive fit is exactly unidentified at one observation per row — NOT FIXED

At exactly one observed cell per row, the alternating-means additive fit drives the row effect to an
exact interpolation of that cell, so every column effect collapses to **exactly zero**:

```
k=1 fitted column effects c = [0. 0. 0. 0. 0. 0. 0.]      max |c| = 0.0
```

`np.argmin` then breaks the resulting all-equal row by column index, so the arm labelled
"fixed ranking" is in fact **"always pick the alphabetically first accelerator"**. On the extended
grid that is A100-40GB, which is optimal for **0 %** of rows:

| always-pick | A100-40GB | A10G | H100 | H200 | L4 | L40S | T4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| regret | **1.8185** | 1.6359 | 1.5049 | 1.4435 | 1.0257 | 1.4127 | 2.0565 |

A uniform-random tie-break would give 1.5568. The reported 1.8185 is near the bad end of the range.

This affects three places:
- `cf_free_embedding.py` — every `k = 1` row (1.8185 / 1.9043 / 2.1193) is this artefact, not a fixed
  ranking.
- `cf_sample_complexity.py` — the "row-mean fill" arm drops the column effect by construction, so it
  is scored as "always A100-40GB" at *every* density (constant 1.8185, table in §2.1).
- `cf_partial_observability.py` — at `k = 0` and `k = 1` all four arms tie at **50.8 %**, i.e. chance.

Not fixed: the replacement tie-break (random, or declaring the quantity unidentified) is a modelling
choice, not a mechanical correction.

**`cf_partial_observability.py` carries two further defects on top of this.**

*H1 is vacuous.* The check reads:

> `[PASS] H1 features without precision sit at the fixed-ranking ceiling: partial 50.8 % vs fixed
> 50.8 % at one observation; Table 5 reports both at 81.7 %`

It passes on `|50.8 − 50.8| < 0.03` while its own detail string asserts the anchor is 81.7 %. The
check confirms two collapsed arms agree with each other, 31 points below the ceiling it names.

*H4's tolerance is on the wrong scale (bug class 4).* H4 exists to catch leakage — "substantially
beating [the full-descriptor model] would suggest leakage" — and its tolerance is 5 points. But the
entire headroom being measured (`full − partial`) is 2.3 points at k = 4. A 5-point tolerance can
never fire. It does not fire, and the script's own recovered-fraction output reads:

```
2 observations per workload: 43%
3 observations per workload: 110%
4 observations per workload: 190%
```

Recovering 190 % of a missing descriptor's value from behaviour alone is the leakage signal H4 was
written to detect. **Root cause (bug class 8, construct mismatch):** at k = 4 of C = 5 exactly one
cell per row is hidden, so every scored pair is (hidden, observed). The hybrid carries a free
per-row parameter `u_i` fitted on the four observed cells, which sharpens its prediction on the
*observed* half of every scored pair; the "full" arm has no `u_i` and is handicapped on exactly those
pairs. The 190 % is that asymmetry, not descriptor recovery.

### 2.4 `cf_multicorpus.py` cold-start regret is not a cold-start measurement — NOT FIXED

`experiments/cf_multicorpus.py:636-640`:

```python
pa = [p for p in pair_records(Y, P_add, range(n), cols_all)[0] if j0 in (p[1], p[2])]   # ACCURACY: restricted
ri = pair_records(Y, P_int, range(n), cols_all)[1]                                       # REGRET: not restricted
```

Accuracy is correctly restricted to pairs involving the held-out platform `j0`. Regret is not: the
`argmin` runs over all 18 platforms, 17 of which the model has measured. Table 9's two columns
therefore describe different choice sets.

I instrumented the function and re-ran it (sanity 56/56, all published values reproduced bit for
bit). Over the 18 Grid'5000 folds:

| | value |
|---|---|
| rows where the model **picks** the held-out platform | **2 of 90 (2.2 %)** |
| rows where the **true** optimum is the held-out platform | 5 of 90 (5.6 %) |
| regret as reported (argmin over all 18) | bilinear **1.0040**, additive 1.0473 |
| regret construct-matched (new device vs model's best seen device) | bilinear **1.0026**, additive **1.0039** |

94 % of the reported regret contains no cold-start decision at all. Construct-matched, the
bilinear-over-additive separation collapses from 0.0433 to **0.0013**, a 33-fold reduction; with the
idle probe added the construct-matched bilinear (1.0040) is marginally *worse* than the
construct-matched additive (1.0039). This is why "16 of the 18 held-out platforms give a regret of
exactly 1.000" — those folds never put the new device in contention.

Corroborating tells in the shipped JSON: `regret_additive` is byte-identical
(`1.047252419369284`) across `grid5000_spec` and `grid5000_spec_plus_idle`, two genuinely different
descriptor sets, and the `montcalm` fold returns that same constant under spec, spec+idle *and* the
permuted control.

Separately, the bilinear's accuracy gain over additive is not significant:
`gain_bootstrap` = +0.066, CI [−1.63, +1.31]. The permuted control (73.9 %) does establish that the
descriptors carry real signal, so the **accuracy** headline stands; the "+bilinear" framing does not.

Not fixed: restricting the regret requires choosing the cold-start decision problem, which is a
research decision.

### 2.7 Measurement-window error in the facility simulators — NOT FIXED

`experiments/fig2_pareto.py:46-49`:

```python
dyn += JOB * E[jk][m] - IDLE[m] * rt          # credits idle for the job's FULL runtime
static = sum(IDLE[s] for s in slots) * HORIZON # charges idle only to t = 3600
```

Jobs start and finish past the horizon, so idle is credited for time that was never charged and
facility energy is systematically **under-counted**. The signature is the impossible-utilisation one
already found once in this project:

| fleet | busy time | slot capacity | utilisation | last job ends |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4×L4 + 6×L40S (the reported optimum) | 34,919 s | 36,000 s | 97.0 % | 3,694 s |
| all-A100 (the reference) | 33,901 s | 36,000 s | 94.2 % | 3,644 s |
| all-T4 | 159,838 s | 36,000 s | **444.0 %** | 16,074 s |

839 of the 1001 plotted fleets breach the SLA and so run past the horizon. I recomputed all 1001 with
idle charged over each slot's own completion time:

| | as shipped | corrected |
|---|---|---|
| Pareto frontier size | **23 non-dominated fleets** | **19** |
| of which SLA-infeasible | 14 | 10 |
| global minimum (all-L4) | 2,492.3 J/job | **3,218.6 J/job (+29 %)** |
| fleets appearing cheaper than the optimum | 295 | 77 |
| SLA-feasible optimum | 4×L4 + 6×L40S, 4,476.3 J/job | **same fleet**, 4,484.2 J/job (+0.18 %) |

**The 34.3 per cent composition headline is safe** — the optimum runs at 97 % utilisation, its
overhang is 342 s out of 34,919 s, and the SLA filter excludes every under-counted fleet from the
argmin. What is wrong is Figure 5 and the two sentences drawn from it: *"a Pareto frontier of 23
non-dominated fleets"* (it is 19) and *"a buyer optimising energy alone will select an infeasible
fleet"* — true, but amplified by a bug that makes infeasible fleets look up to 29 % cheaper than they
are.

**The same defect appears in nine other simulators**, and it is the single root cause behind three
separate anomalies found in this audit. Measured overhangs (uniform mix, seed 0):

| fleet / setting | makespan | energy understated by |
|---|---|---|
| `4xL4+6xL40S` (the SLA-feasible optimum) | 3,669 s | 0.6 % |
| `5xL4+5xL40S` | 3,822 s | 1.8 % |
| `10xL4` (the transformer-mix "optimum") | 7,830 s | **28.8 %** |
| `b7_assignment_baseline.py` pool at λ = 4 | **39,937 s** | **11× undercount of static energy** |
| `e6_contention.py` at λ ≤ 0.2 / λ = 0.4 | — | ≤0.1 % / ~4 % |

The three anomalies it explains:
1. **Regret below 1.0**, which is impossible against an oracle: `b3_mix_inference.json` reports
   `/summary[0]/regret` = 0.9851 and `b4_active_probing.json` 0.9880. Both carry
   `sla_violation_rate` 0.4–0.6 and a separate `regret_feasible_only` above 1.0. An SLA-breaching
   fleet cannot really beat the constrained oracle on energy per job; it only appears to because its
   post-horizon runtime is credited idle that was never charged.
2. **Figure 5's low-energy tail** (this section).
3. **`b7_assignment_baseline.py`'s energy per job falling monotonically with load** — the giveaway,
   since more load cannot make a facility more efficient per job without bound.

Affected: `b3_mix_inference.py`, `b4_active_probing.py`, `b5_shapley_sla_sweep.py`,
`b7_assignment_baseline.py`, `fleet_vs_mix.py`, `shapley_fleet.py`, `k6_seed_variance.py`,
`e6_contention.py`, `n_fixes.py`. Unaffected: `g2_refresh.py` and `g6_utilisation.py` are
makespan-aware; `b8_stochastic_program.py` and `g4_cost_sharing.py` charge idle without the credit.

For SLA-feasible headline fleets the effect is under 2 %. For every infeasible fleet it is large.

Not fixed: the accounting window is a modelling choice (charge idle to makespan, or truncate jobs at
the horizon), so the correction is quantified rather than applied — and applying it moves every
reported J/job number across six scripts.

### 2.8 `b7_assignment_baseline.py`: the joint-vs-greedy comparison is degenerate — NOT FIXED

`b7_assignment.json` reports `joint_gain_pct = 0.0` with `greedy_true == joint_true` to 15
significant digits at all five λ, and the decomposition says "policy alone: 0.0 J/job, it is all
prediction". Instrumenting the scheduler shows why: **`k = min(len(queue), len(free))` equals 1 at
100 % of epochs at every λ** (324 to 14,533 epochs each). At low load a job arrives to an idle fleet;
at high load the loop advances `now` to the next machine-free time, which frees exactly one slot. The
Hungarian solve is always a 1 × 1 problem and is *identically* greedy. The invariant "joint never
loses to greedy" passes vacuously.

Secondarily, `linear_sum_assignment` minimises `cost_table` (energy per sample) while the reported
objective is `JOB*E − IDLE*rt`, so even with k > 1 the two arms would not be construct-matched.

The paper's *"0.0 J per job attributable to policy and 13.1 J per job to prediction"* rests on this.
The 0.0 is an artefact of a scheduler that never has a choice to make, not a measurement. Not fixed:
any repair is a redesign (batching arrivals is what the previous version did, and that was
independently wrong).

### 2.9 `_patch_b8.py` is a regression landmine — NOT FIXED

`experiments/_patch_b8.py` rewrites `b8_stochastic_program.py` from a stored `TAIL` string. That TAIL
contains the **pre-fix** `local_search`, verified at `_patch_b8.py:44-66`:

```python
if cv < v - 1e-9:
    v, n, improved = cv, cand, True     # applied MID-SCAN, inside the a/b loops
```

with no `assert cand.min() >= 0`. The current `b8_stochastic_program.py:229-243` carries the fix
(steepest descent, moves applied after the full scan, asserts present) and a comment recording that
the old version "produc[ed] negative counts and fleets that spuriously beat exhaustive enumeration" —
bug class 6. The TAIL also drops the `outsourced < 1e-9` filter from the S2 fidelity set.

Running `_patch_b8.py` silently reverts the file that produces the paper's VSS 21.3 % and EVPI 16.8 %.
Recommend deleting it or renaming it `.obsolete`. Not done here: removing a file from the user's tree
is outside this audit's remit.

Every interaction share in the paper reproduces exactly from its stored matrix, and every
decomposition sums to 100.0000:

| paper | source | value | shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.5 % | `results/interaction_matrix.json` → `/interaction_share_pct` | 2.4996 | 24 × 5 |
| 2.49 % | `results/ejhusom_corpus.json` → `/shares/interaction` | 2.4938 | **5 × 2** |
| 2.2 % | `results/k2_grid5000.json` → `/shares/interaction` | 2.1601 | 5 × 18 |
| 2.66 % | `results/wilkins_corpus.json` → `/shares/interaction` | 2.6626 | 32 × 4 |
| 9.0 % | `results/j2_llmperf_matrix.json` → `/shares/interaction` | 8.9796 | 74 × 3 |
| **0.3 %** | **none** | — | **untraceable, see §3** |

The statistic depends strongly on how many columns are included, so the cross-corpus comparison is

```
wilkins  32x4  full 2.66%   2-column sub-matrices: min 0.43  median 1.09  max 6.31
llm-perf 74x3  full 8.98%   2-column sub-matrices: min 2.76  median 6.25  max 11.03
grid5000 5x18  full 2.16%   2-column sub-matrices: min 0.01  median 0.83  max 5.42  (n=153)
```

A two-column reading of Grid'5000 can be anything from 0.01 % to 5.42 %. The paper's *"the
interaction is 2.49 per cent, within 0.01 percentage points of our own"* is a coincidence between a
5 × 2 matrix and a 24 × 5 one, not a replication. On a two-column matrix `res[i][0] = −res[i][1]`
exactly, so residual correlation is forced to ±1; the codebase knows this — `ejhusom_corpus.py`
replaced its permutation null for precisely this reason and `cf_multicorpus.py` marks the corpus

(31 + 3 + 93 = 127 = 128 − 1 on Wilkins; 4 + 17 + 68 = 89 = 90 − 1 on Grid'5000; 4 + 1 + 4 = 9 = 10 − 1
on ejhusom). The interaction bucket *is* the saturated residual, so it contains all measurement noise.
Every reported interaction share is an upper bound.
